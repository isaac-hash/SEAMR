# **Product Concept: A Data-Driven Self-Improvement & Wellbeing System**

**Working concept: LIFEOS — Treat your life like a performance system.**

An application that helps people measure, analyse and improve their personal development through data. It combines habit tracking, fitness tracking, learning, time management, calendar integration and personal analytics into one system.

The central philosophy:

> Don't just feel like you're making progress. Measure it. Analyse it. Improve it by 1% every day.

The app should essentially function like an **Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) dashboard for your life**, turning everyday activities into measurable insights.

# **5. The 1% Improvement & Drawdown System**

This is potentially the most distinctive feature of the application.

Rather than simply counting completed habits, the app should analyse the user's performance over time.

### **A. The 1% improvement principle**

Track changes across daily, weekly and monthly periods.

For example:

| Metric            | Last week | This week | Change |
| ----------------- | --------- | --------- | ------ |
| Pages read/day    | 20        | 22        | +10%   |
| Gym reps/week     | 400       | 440       | +10%   |
| French hours/week | 4         | 5         | +25%   |
| Sport hours/week  | 6         | 5         | -16.7% |
| Words learnt/week | 50        | 55        | +10%   |

The app should automatically calculate percentage changes.

\\[ \text{Growth}=\frac{\text{Current}-\text{Previous}}{\text{Previous}}\times100 \\]

### **B. Performance drawdown**

Borrow an idea from financial markets.

In trading, drawdown measures how far an investment has fallen from its previous peak.

In LIFEOS, drawdown could measure how far a user's activity or performance has fallen from their personal best.

Example:

- Highest weekly reading average: 35 pages/day.
- Current weekly average: 25 pages/day.
- Drawdown: -28.6%.

This helps identify when a user is falling away from their previous performance.

### **C. Performance alerts**

The app could automatically highlight:

-

  Improving

  &#x20;French learning increased 15%.


-

  Declining

  &#x20;Sport participation decreased 20%.


-

  At risk

  &#x20;Reading consistency has declined for three consecutive weeks.


-

  Milestone

  &#x20;Gym strength reached a new personal best.



One important distinction: not every decrease is bad. Rest, recovery and reduced workload can be healthy. The system should understand targets and context rather than treating every metric as something to maximise.

Users can define days when particular activities must happen.

For example:

- Tuesday: French.
- Wednesday: Sport.
- Thursday: Gym.
- Friday: French.
- Saturday: Sport and reading.
- Sunday: Recovery and weekly review.

The system should respect these commitments before filling remaining available time.

It should also distinguish between fixed commitments, flexible activities and protected rest time.

# **7. Wellbeing & Recovery**

I would make this a core module, not an afterthought.

A self-improvement system can become counterproductive if users feel compelled to optimise every minute.

Track:

- Sleep duration and quality.
- Daily energy.
- Mood.
- Stress.
- Recovery days.
- Workload.
- Subjective wellbeing.

The app should be able to recognise patterns such as:

"You have increased your training volume by 30% over the last three weeks, while your reported energy has decreased."

Or:

"Your most productive learning sessions occur on days when you sleep longer."

This is where the application starts providing genuinely useful personal insights.

For example:

- Do you read more on days you exercise?
- Does your French learning improve when you practise in the morning?
- Does your sleep quality correlate with gym performance?
- Do you become less consistent when your calendar is overloaded?

Rather than just tracking activities, the app starts discovering patterns in the user's behaviour.

# **11. Product Differentiation & Positioning**

There are plenty of habit trackers, fitness apps and productivity tools. The opportunity is to bring their capabilities together into one coherent personal analytics system.

I would position the product around three principles:

# **01**

**Measure everything that matters**

Turn personal development into measurable activities and outcomes.

# **02**

**Make time for what matters**

Use intelligent scheduling to ensure that personal development actually happens.

# **03**

**Improve through analysis**

Use historical data, trends and feedback to make better decisions.

# **12. Recommended MVP (First Version)**

I would resist building all seven modules immediately. Start with the core system that proves the concept.

| Priority | Feature                              | MVP       |
| -------- | ------------------------------------ | --------- |
| 1        | Custom activity tracker              | Essential |
| 2        | Goals and targets                    | Essential |
| 3        | Daily dashboard                      | Essential |
| 4        | Weekly analytics and charts          | Essential |
| 5        | Percentage improvement and drawdown  | Essential |
| 6        | Weekly reports                       | Essential |
| 7        | Google Calendar integration          | Phase 2   |
| 8        | Outlook integration                  | Phase 2   |
| 9        | Intelligent scheduling               | Phase 2   |
| 10       | Wellbeing analytics and correlations | Phase 3   |

**My recommendation:** The product's real competitive advantage should be its personal analytics engine, not its habit tracker.

Most apps tell people whether they completed a habit. This app should tell them whether their behaviour is producing measurable improvement, where their performance is deteriorating, how they're allocating their time, and what they should change.

The ultimate product vision:

> **A personal operating system that turns your life into measurable data, uses that data to understand your behaviour, and intelligently allocates your time to help you become the person you want to be.**

A strong next step would be to turn this into a proper product requirements document (PRD), including the complete screen-by-screen UX, data model, analytics formulas and AI scheduling logic.
